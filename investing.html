Investing Guides

Index funds, robo-advisors, risk by age, and what market swings actually mean for your money — without the jargon.

Investing well doesn't require picking stocks, timing the market, or understanding derivatives — for most people, it means putting money into low-cost index funds on a regular schedule and leaving it alone. This section explains what index funds actually are and why they beat most alternatives for ordinary savers, how to decide between a robo-advisor and managing investments yourself, and how much investment risk actually makes sense at your age and timeline rather than a generic rule of thumb. It also covers what to do — and, just as importantly, what not to do — when a recession or a market drop makes your retirement account balance fall, since panic-selling during a downturn is one of the most common and costly investing mistakes people make.
