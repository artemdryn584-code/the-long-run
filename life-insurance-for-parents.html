Life Insurance Basics for Parents: What You Actually Need

Sept 12, 2026, 6 min read

Life insurance is one of those purchases most parents know they should make and then put off, partly because the options are confusing on purpose and partly because it's not a fun way to spend an evening. The good news is that for most families, the right answer is simpler and cheaper than the sales pitch suggests.

Term life is the right starting point for almost every parent

Term life insurance covers you for a set period — commonly 10, 20, or 30 years — and pays out only if you die during that term. It's dramatically cheaper than permanent (whole or universal) life insurance for the same coverage amount, because it isn't also functioning as a savings or investment product. For a parent whose main goal is replacing income and covering a mortgage or childcare costs until kids are grown, term life does that job directly.

Where whole life sometimes fits

Whole life insurance lasts your entire life and builds cash value, but it costs significantly more per dollar of coverage. It can make sense in specific situations — certain estate planning needs, or a family member with a lifelong dependent — but for most parents just trying to protect their kids' financial future, the extra cost buys less coverage than the same budget spent on term life plus separate investing. If you haven't built a basic emergency fund yet, that comes before either type of policy.

A simple way to estimate how much coverage you need

  • Add up major future costs: remaining mortgage, years of childcare or private school, and a reasonable estimate of college costs.
  • Add 5-10 years of your income, to replace what your family would lose while adjusting.
  • Subtract existing savings, retirement accounts, and any life insurance already provided through work.

The result is a rough but realistic coverage target — often 10-15 times annual income for parents with young children, though the actual number depends heavily on debt, savings, and how many years of support you're planning to replace.

The goal isn't to cover every possible cost forever. It's to give your family enough time and money to adjust without a second crisis on top of the first.

Both parents usually need coverage, not just the higher earner

A parent who doesn't work for pay, or earns less, still provides real economic value — childcare, household management, and logistics that would otherwise cost real money to replace. Skipping coverage for that parent because "they don't have an income" is one of the most common gaps in family life insurance planning.

What to actually do

  1. Get quotes for a term policy matching your years remaining until kids are financially independent (often 20-30 years).
  2. Use the rough coverage estimate above as a starting point, then adjust for your actual debts and goals.
  3. Get policies for both parents if both provide financial or caregiving value to the household.
  4. Revisit coverage after a new child, a new mortgage, or a significant income change — not just once and never again.
