Renting vs. Buying in 2026: The Math Most Calculators Get Wrong

Aug 21, 2026, 9 min read

Most rent-vs-buy comparisons stop at "your mortgage payment vs. your rent payment." That's an incomplete picture, because it ignores what your money would be doing if you didn't put it into a house.

The real cost of buying isn't just the mortgage

Property taxes, homeowners insurance, maintenance (often estimated at 1-2% of the home's value per year), and closing costs when you eventually sell all belong in the comparison. A mortgage payment that looks similar to rent on paper is often meaningfully higher once these are added.

The part almost every calculator skips: opportunity cost

A down payment and any monthly difference between renting and owning could instead be invested. If buying costs $500 a month more than renting, and that $500 could have gone into an index fund, the comparison isn't just rent vs. mortgage — it's rent-plus-investing vs. owning.

Where buying tends to win

  • You plan to stay in the home long enough (often 5+ years) to absorb the up-front closing costs.
  • Local rent is rising quickly while a fixed-rate mortgage would lock in a stable payment.
  • You value the non-financial parts of ownership — stability, the ability to renovate, not sharing walls with a landlord's decisions.

Where renting tends to win

  • You expect to move within the next few years, which doesn't leave time to recoup closing costs.
  • Home prices in your area are high relative to rent (a high "price-to-rent ratio").
  • You'd rather keep your savings liquid and invested rather than tied up in a single property.
The right answer depends on how long you'll stay and what else your money could be doing — not just which monthly payment looks smaller.

A more honest way to compare

Look up your local price-to-rent ratio (home price divided by annual rent for a similar property). As a rough guide, ratios under about 15 tend to favor buying, and ratios above about 20 tend to favor renting — though local market conditions, how long you'll stay, and your own finances still matter more than any single number.
