Robo-Advisors for Beginners: What to Look For

Sept 12, 2026, 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Compare total cost, not just the headline fee — the management fee (commonly 0.25%-0.50%) plus any underlying fund expense ratios.
  • Account minimums range from none to a few thousand dollars, so confirm one before assuming a service is an option.
  • Check which account types are supported: taxable brokerage, Traditional and Roth IRAs, and 401(k) rollovers.
  • Confirm whether tax-loss harvesting is included and at what tier, especially if you'll invest in a taxable account.

If you've already weighed robo-advisors against managing your own index funds and decided a robo-advisor fits how you want to invest, the next question is more concrete: what actually separates one robo-advisor from another. Specific pricing and features change often enough that naming a single "best" one goes stale fast — this is what to actually check before you sign up for any of them.

The management fee, and what it actually covers

Most robo-advisors charge an annual fee as a percentage of your balance, commonly in the range of 0.25%-0.50%. Confirm whether that fee is the entire cost, or whether the underlying funds it invests in also carry their own separate expense ratios on top — the combined cost is what actually matters, not just the headline number.

Account minimums

Some robo-advisors have no minimum to open an account, while others require a few hundred to a few thousand dollars to start. If you're beginning with a small amount, confirm the minimum before assuming a particular service is an option.

Which account types are actually supported

  • Taxable brokerage accounts: Standard investing accounts with no special tax treatment.
  • IRAs (Traditional and Roth): Confirm both are offered if you want tax-advantaged retirement investing through the same platform.
  • 401(k) rollovers: Some robo-advisors specifically support rolling over an old employer 401(k); others don't handle this directly.

Tax-loss harvesting: check if it's actually included

This feature — automatically selling losing positions in a taxable account to offset gains elsewhere — is one of the more genuinely valuable things a robo-advisor can do that's tedious to replicate manually. Some providers include it standard, others only on premium tiers, and some don't offer it at all. If you'll be investing in a taxable account, this is worth specifically confirming.

Two robo-advisors charging the same headline fee can end up costing very different amounts once minimums, fund expense ratios, and premium-tier features are factored in.

How much control you actually have over the portfolio

Some services let you adjust the underlying fund mix or exclude certain sectors; others are fully automated with no customization. Neither approach is wrong, but it's worth knowing which one you're getting — a fully hands-off design is the point for some people and a dealbreaker for others.

A short checklist before signing up

  1. Total annual cost: management fee plus underlying fund expense ratios.
  2. Minimum balance to open and to access any premium features.
  3. Account types supported (taxable, Roth IRA, Traditional IRA, 401(k) rollover).
  4. Whether tax-loss harvesting is included, and at what balance or tier.
  5. How easy it is to withdraw or transfer out if you change your mind later.

Since fees and features change, check the provider's current pricing page directly rather than relying on an older comparison — including this one.
