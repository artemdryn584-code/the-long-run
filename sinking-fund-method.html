The Sinking Fund Method: How to Stop Dreading Car Repairs and Holidays

Sept 12, 2026, 5 min read

Car repairs, holiday gifts, annual insurance premiums, back-to-school costs — none of these are actually surprises. They happen every year, on a schedule you can mostly predict, and yet they routinely land on a credit card because there was no separate money set aside for them. A sinking fund fixes exactly this gap.

What a sinking fund actually is

A sinking fund is a small savings account (or a labeled bucket within one) for a specific, known future expense — separate from your emergency fund, which exists for genuinely unplanned events. The difference matters: an emergency fund is for the unknown, a sinking fund is for the known-but-not-monthly.

How to set one up

  1. List your irregular but predictable costs from the past year: car maintenance, gifts, an annual subscription, holiday travel.
  2. Estimate a yearly total for each, then divide by 12 to get a monthly savings target.
  3. Open a separate savings account (or use "buckets" if your bank supports them) and automate a small transfer for each category on payday.

Why separating the money matters

Lumping all savings into one account makes it easy to accidentally spend the money meant for December gifts on something else in July. Separate, labeled sinking funds create a visible boundary — the "car repair" bucket only gets touched for car repairs, which keeps the actual balance meaningful instead of just a vague cushion.

A sinking fund doesn't create new money. It just moves an expense you already knew was coming from "surprise" to "planned."

Common sinking funds worth starting with

  • Car maintenance and repairs
  • Holiday and gift spending
  • Annual or semi-annual insurance premiums
  • Home maintenance (a leaking faucet is cheaper to fix than to ignore)
  • Travel and vacations

Start with one

You don't need five sinking funds running at once to get the benefit. Pick the expense that's burned you the most in the past year, calculate a monthly number, and automate it this week. Once it's running on its own, adding a second one takes a few minutes.
