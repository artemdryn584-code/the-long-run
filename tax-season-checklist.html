Tax Season Checklist: What to Gather Before You File

Sept 12, 2026, 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Gather income documents — W-2s, 1099s, and records of any other income — before you sit down to file.
  • Collect deduction and credit paperwork: mortgage interest statements, student loan interest statements, childcare provider info, and donation receipts.
  • Traditional IRA contributions can often still be made up until the filing deadline and counted for the prior tax year — check if you're close to a contribution limit.
  • Keep Social Security numbers, bank/routing numbers, and last year's return on hand, and start a folder each January to make next year easier.

Tax season — roughly late January through the mid-April filing deadline — is when a little upfront organization saves the most time. Most of the stress of filing comes from hunting for documents at the last minute, not from the actual filing itself. Gathering everything below before you sit down to file turns a multi-day scramble into an afternoon.

Income documents

  • W-2s from every employer you worked for during the year, including part-time or short-term jobs.
  • 1099 forms for freelance income, contract work, interest, dividends, or retirement account distributions — these come from whoever paid you, not from you.
  • Records of any other income not covered by a standard form, such as rental income or side income paid directly rather than through a platform that issues a 1099.

Deduction and credit paperwork

  • Mortgage interest statements (Form 1098), if you own a home.
  • Student loan interest statements, if you paid interest during the year.
  • Childcare provider information, including their tax ID, if claiming a dependent care credit.
  • Receipts for deductible expenses if you're itemizing rather than taking the standard deduction.
  • Records of charitable donations, including receipts for anything beyond small cash gifts.

Retirement and health account numbers

Contributions to a Traditional IRA can sometimes still be made up until the filing deadline and counted for the prior tax year — worth checking if you're close to a contribution limit and want the deduction. HSA contribution records also matter if you have a health savings account, since contributions there are tax-advantaged as well.

Tax season isn't actually complicated for most people. It's just scattered across a dozen documents that show up in different places throughout January and February.

Personal information worth having on hand

  • Social Security numbers for yourself, your spouse, and any dependents.
  • Bank account and routing numbers for direct deposit of any refund.
  • Last year's tax return, which most filing software or preparers use as a reference starting point.

A simple way to stay ahead of it next year

Creating a single folder — physical or digital — right when tax documents start arriving in January, and dropping each form into it as it comes, turns filing into gathering one folder instead of reconstructing a year of paperwork from memory.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start gathering my tax documents?

As soon as they start arriving, typically in January — most forms like W-2s and 1099s are required to be sent by the end of January, so setting up a folder then and adding each document as it comes in avoids a last-minute scramble before the mid-April deadline.

What documents do I need if I'm self-employed or did freelance work?

You'll need 1099 forms from anyone who paid you, plus your own records of any income not covered by a 1099, such as direct payments or rental income.

Can I still contribute to an IRA and have it count for last year's taxes?

Yes — contributions to a Traditional IRA can often be made up until the filing deadline and still counted toward the prior tax year, which is worth checking if you're close to a contribution limit and want the deduction.

What if I'm itemizing deductions instead of taking the standard deduction?

Keep receipts for deductible expenses and records of charitable donations beyond small cash gifts, along with mortgage interest statements (Form 1098) and student loan interest statements.

What personal information should I have ready before filing?

Social Security numbers for yourself, your spouse, and any dependents, your bank account and routing numbers for direct deposit of a refund, and last year's tax return as a reference.
